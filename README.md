## open source app
# **openRung**
OpenRung is an open-source application that believes in the right to free access to the internet, and that no firewall should block people from all over the world. OpenRung aims to provide VPN services to residents of Iran and mainland China to gain access to the internet. It can also be used if your country has censorship, such as Russia.
# **proton vpn**
Proton VPN is an open-source VPN created by Swedish scientists. It boasts over 20,000 nodes across more than 100 countries, and you can even download configuration files from the official website to manually connect to different regions (although due to regional restrictions, it's practically impossible to connect without adding special rules). However, it's a paid service; the free version only supports about 10 countries and doesn't allow for free country selection, which might make your IP address less stable and reduce your trust level. (However, there is a workaround: if you connect to another VPN and then reconnect to Proton VPN, and then if you don't want to use Proton VPN or are temporarily unavailable, you can reconnect to another VPN you previously connected to, and then reconnect to Proton VPN to ensure a stable node. However, clearing the background or accidentally disconnecting the VPN connection will cause it to fail.) Proton also has many derivative projects, such as email and cloud storage, which you can try. Despite this, it's a very established service with many proprietary rules against regional restrictions, making it almost unreliable and the king of free VPNs (and there may be other tiers above it...).

# **Grep**(also called“迷雾通”)
Grep is an open-source project, its core highlight being its use of the GUN license. This means you can release a cracked version, but you must also release the source code. For details, please refer to the definition of GUN. Grep also has a paid version and a regular version. The regular version only supports 3G networks and is limited to web browsing; even downloading an APK is difficult. The paid version unlocks more nodes, has lower network purity and less network pressure, but it's not as established or user-friendly, so it's relegated to this position for now.

# **WARP(1.1.1.1)**
WARP (1.1.1.1), produced by Cloudflare, is hereinafter referred to as 1.1.1.1. 1.1.1.1 has two modes: a standard mode accessible to everyone and a paid mode. In either mode, you cannot freely choose your nodes; Cloudflare only selects nodes based on their proximity. For example, if you want to access YouTube, it might choose a node that YouTube doesn't accept for acceleration. There are also configuration pitfalls. If you only enable DNS encryption (in 1.1.1.1 mode), it will only encrypt your DNS, and you still won't be able to access the external network—essentially operating without any protection! While using WARP mode allows access to the external network, the nodes are not fixed. It's okay for temporary use, but not for daily use. Many YouTubers praise its capabilities, but I think its performance is about average, similar to Lantern. The main issue is the unfixed node configuration, which I dislike.

# other countries vpn
## Hello vpn
Hello VPN offers connections to 8 countries, although 4 of them are frequently disconnected (perhaps due to expired servers or inaccessibility). However, its internet speed is extremely high, entirely dependent on your network bandwidth. If you have a server-level connection of around 500MB, it can perform perfectly. For ordinary users, it's good for downloading, watching YouTube videos, and unlocking Netflix. However, for some reason, the speed is inconsistent. It works well as a backup for downloading or browsing GitHub and Pornhub, hence it's the first VPN introduced among those for other countries.
## Finevpn
FineVPN has many nodes. Accessing the configuration file for the first time might take a while, but you can start using it afterward. I vaguely remember it requiring a VIP membership, but there are also many free nodes. It even has nodes in China, meaning you can use it to access Chinese apps like TikTok, WeChat, and QQ. However, compared to a VPN for accessing Chinese websites, it's still just average. Given that it has nodes in China, it's placed after HelloVPN.
## vpn super unlimited proxy
Here we'll refer to it simply as Super VPN. Super VPN is a fairly standard VPN; it has Japanese nodes, but a VIP membership is required. Although cracked versions exist, they are suspected of containing viruses. It's advisable to download a legitimate version as a backup.
## Tempovpn
Tempovpn, despite its name, is essentially an "airport." It uses tools like Shadowsocks to collect publicly available nodes online, then packages them into an application for others to use. While it's free, it has ads, and although everything works, it's prone to crashing, so I don't recommend using it.
